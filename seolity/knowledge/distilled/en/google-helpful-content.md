# Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content

Google's ranking systems prioritize helpful, reliable content made to benefit people rather than to manipulate rankings. This guidance covers self-assessing content quality, demonstrating E-E-A-T, being transparent about who, how and why content was created, and avoiding search engine-first practices.

## Self-assess content quality

- Do evaluate each page against quality questions: does it offer original information, reporting, research or analysis, and a substantial, complete description of the topic? Pages that merely restate the obvious offer little value.
- Do add substantial value and originality when drawing on other sources. Avoid simply copying or rewriting them, because attribution does not replace original effort.
- Do compare your page with other pages in search results and make sure it provides substantial additional value.
- Do write a main heading or page title that is descriptive and helpful. Avoid exaggerated or shocking titles because they misrepresent the content.
- Do proofread for spelling and stylistic issues and avoid sloppy or hastily produced content.
- Avoid mass-producing content, outsourcing it to a large number of creators, or spreading it across a network of sites, because individual pages then get little attention or care.
- Do ask people you trust but who are unaffiliated with your site for an honest assessment of your content.
- Do audit traffic drops: identify which pages and search types were most affected and assess those pages against the quality questions.

## Build people-first content

- Do create content primarily for an existing or intended audience that would find it useful if they came directly to your site.
- Do demonstrate first-hand expertise and depth of knowledge, such as having actually used a product or service or visited a place.
- Do keep your site focused on a primary purpose or topic area.
- Do make sure readers leave feeling they learned enough to achieve their goal and had a satisfying experience.
- Avoid search engine-first content: producing lots of content on many topics hoping some performs, relying on extensive automation, or mainly summarizing others without adding value.
- Avoid writing about trending topics only because they are trending when you would not otherwise write about them for your audience.
- Avoid entering a niche without real expertise just to get search traffic.
- Avoid targeting a particular word count, because Google has no preferred word count.
- Avoid promising answers to questions that have no answer, such as a release date that has not been confirmed.
- Avoid changing page dates to look fresh when the content has not substantially changed, and avoid adding or removing large amounts of content just to make the site seem fresh, because it will not help rankings.
- Do apply SEO to people-first content; SEO that helps search engines discover and understand your content is helpful, but it should not replace creating content for people.

## Demonstrate E-E-A-T

- Do show trust signals: clear sourcing, evidence of expertise, and background on the author and the site, such as links to an author page or an About page.
- Do have content written or reviewed by an expert or enthusiast who demonstrably knows the topic, and avoid easily verified factual errors.
- Do prioritize trust, the most important E-E-A-T aspect; content does not need to show every aspect, since experience or expertise alone can make content helpful.
- Do apply especially strong E-E-A-T and high accuracy to YMYL topics (health, financial stability, safety, societal welfare), and keep content consistent with established expert consensus.
- Do read the search quality rater guidelines to self-assess content, remembering that E-E-A-T is not a specific ranking factor and rater data does not directly affect rankings.

## Strengthen main content

- Do treat main content as everything that directly helps the page achieve its purpose: primary text and media, interactive features such as calculators or tools, relevant user-generated contributions, tabbed or expanded sections, and titles and headings.
- Do invest real human effort in content; avoid automatically generating pages from feeds or using generative AI to produce large amounts of text without manual oversight or curation.
- Do offer unique information or perspectives that are not already available on other websites.
- Do demonstrate the skill needed for a satisfying experience, such as clear writing, well-produced video or functional page tools, noting that not all content requires specialized expertise.
- Do keep informational content factually accurate.
- Do provide an overall great page experience across many aspects rather than focusing on only one or two.

## Who: make authorship clear

- Do make it self-evident who created the content, and add accurate bylines where readers would expect them.
- Do link bylines to author information giving background on the author and the areas they write about.
- Avoid deceptive authorship, such as fabricated creator profiles with AI-generated headshots, made-up names or false credentials, because deception makes a page untrustworthy and signals low quality.

## How and why: be transparent about creation

- Do explain how content was produced where readers might wonder, for example for product reviews state how many products were tested, how tests were conducted and the results, with evidence such as photographs.
- Do disclose substantial use of automation or AI-generation where it would reasonably be expected, and explain how and why it was used.
- Do create content primarily to help people, so that it is useful to visitors who come to your site directly.
- Avoid using automation, including AI-generation, to produce content primarily to manipulate search rankings, because it violates Google's spam policies.
